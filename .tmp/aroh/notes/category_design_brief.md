## Working Big Idea (draft, for Section 00)

> **Wake up knowing what matters, why it matters, who needs to act — and what Aroh has already done about it.**

Adapted from the S1 promise. Ogilvy test: one sentence, a promise the reader can check every morning.

## From → To (draft — to confirm at sign-off)

| Old way | New way, with Owner Intelligence |
| ------- | ---------------------------------- |
| The owner wakes up asking "what am I missing?" | Aroh opens at 07:30 with the three things that matter today |
| Updates arrive whenever someone remembers | Exceptions surface the moment a threshold is crossed |
| The owner chases follow-ups by phone and WhatsApp | Aroh closes the loop and reports back at 18:30 |
| Five systems, five versions of the number | One answer, with its source shown |
| Dashboards the owner has to read | A brief that reads the dashboards for them |
| Delegation depends on memory | Every commitment is tracked through to its outcome |

## Sutherland notes to carry into later sections

- **Anxiety reduction is the product** (Uber map principle) — headline benefit is calm, not hours saved.
- **Doorman fallacy risk** — a human chief of staff also provides judgment, discretion and loyalty. Section 03 must show how Aroh keeps those: approvals before action, provenance on every claim, configurable autonomy.
- **Price as signal** — an org-level fee signals an executive product; ₹500/seat would signal a utility.
- **Remove buyer blame** — India hosting, approval gates, paid pilot with a defined business loop, references from known promoters.

## Ogilvy notes

- Specific facts available: 07:30 brief / 18:30 close-out; 432 requests/day from the busiest user; 40-hour onboarding; approval card before every change.
- Avoid: "AI-powered" in headlines, "seamless", "revolutionary".
