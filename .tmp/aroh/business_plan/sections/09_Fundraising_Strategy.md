# Fundraising: Fund the Pre-Launch, Then Raise on Proof

> **Last Updated:** 2026-10-25
> **Project:** Aroh
> **Status:** Draft — generated from the model by `write_section09.py`

## Executive Summary

Aroh's funding plan follows its sales plan. **DEWiN's existing shareholders put in ₹5 Cr now** to bridge the five-month pre-launch period — about ₹3.72 Cr of costs fall due before the March 2027 customer payments arrive — without bringing in outside investors [[1]](#ref-1). If fifty owners sign paid contracts by March 2027, Aroh bills about **₹13.68 Cr upfront** and reaches about **₹10.3 Cr of annual recurring revenue (~$1.1M) before its first full financial year**; from then on customer prepayments fund the business and cash never falls below ₹15.0 Cr [[2]](#ref-2).

The seed round is therefore raised **after** the milestone, on evidence, at a valuation set by paying customers — or not at all if cash flow covers growth. This reverses the usual order deliberately. Before any customer exists, the only Indian valuation range found for a seed round puts ₹48 Cr pre-money in its upper half [[3]](#ref-3); with ₹10.3 Cr of ARR, even the median public-software multiple of 4.4× implies about ₹45 Cr [[4]](#ref-4) — before any credit for growth.

The risk the plan funds against is that the fifty contracts do not arrive. Then the business follows the previous plan, which needs about **₹6.0 Cr** before it turns self-funding [[2]](#ref-2). The answer is a seed of about ₹5 Cr raised at the start of FY2027–28 — below the median Indian seed round of ₹8.0 Cr [[5]](#ref-5) — together with holding the development and operations cloud to half its base budget until customers justify it. That keeps cash above ₹4.0 Cr in the downside.

The ₹5 Cr covers the pre-launch costs with about ₹1.28 Cr to spare — enough to run the ten pilots, complete security certification and move AI processing into India before the first paid contract. The fifty direct contracts are onboarded in March and billed annually upfront, so ₹13.68 Cr of customer cash arrives around 31 March — but only if customers pay on time. Recognised revenue in the five-month stub is just ₹0.78 Cr; the rest sits in deferred revenue and is recognised through FY2027–28.

## The seed is raised after the milestone, on evidence

| | Raise the seed now (previous plan) | Raise after March 2027 (this plan) |
| - | ---------------------------------- | ---------------------------------- |
| Evidence investors see | A running product and measured usage | ~57 paying customers, ~₹10.3 Cr ARR, pilot conversion, onboarding hours |
| Valuation basis | Upper half of the only Indian seed range found (₹25–70 Cr) [[3]](#ref-3) | ARR multiples — 4.4× median public software gives ~₹45 Cr before growth [[4]](#ref-4) |
| Dilution for ₹12 Cr | 20% at ₹48 Cr pre-money | Lower, and only if the money is needed |
| If the milestone is missed | Money already raised | Raise ~₹5 Cr at a lower valuation, sized to the downside; agent cloud held at half budget |

Sutherland's point about costly signals applies to investors as much as customers [[6]](#ref-6): fifty owners paying ₹24L each is a far stronger signal than any projection, and it is the signal this plan chooses to raise on.

**Two honest gaps.** Retention is below the Bessemer median because one organisation-level price leaves less room to expand than per-seat pricing. And Aroh grows like a careful enterprise software company, not like the fastest AI companies Bessemer tracks.

## A Series A is optional growth capital

The median US Series A company shows about $3M of ARR [[13]](#ref-13) — about ₹28.5 Cr. Aroh's base case passes that at the end of FY2027–28 (29.0 Cr at March 2028; 61.1 Cr at March 2029), already profitable. A Series A would fund faster expansion, not survival. The median Indian Series A is about ₹50.8 Cr [[5]](#ref-5).

### Key Insights

- DEWiN's existing shareholders bridge the pre-launch with ₹5 Cr; no outside dilution before the March 2027 milestone [[1]](#ref-1).
- If ~57 customers sign by March 2027, the base case funds itself; cash never falls below ₹15.0 Cr [[2]](#ref-2).
- The seed is raised on proof — ~₹10.3 Cr of ARR is worth about ₹45 Cr even at the public-software median multiple [[4]](#ref-4).
- The downside (no pre-launch customers) needs ~₹6.0 Cr; a ~₹5 Cr seed, below the Indian median of ₹8.0 Cr [[5]](#ref-5), covers it with the agent cloud held to half budget.

### Investment Implications

The plan asks investors to buy into evidence, not promises. By the time a seed round is priced, Aroh will either have about fifty paying customers and a business that funds itself — in which case outside capital buys speed, at a valuation set by revenue — or it will not, in which case the downside is known, costed and covered by a round of median size. Either way the existing owners carry the pre-launch risk — the strongest signal of conviction the plan can send that Owner Intelligence is a category worth owning.
