# Customer Validation: What Is Proven, What Is Not, and How the Pilots Will Tell Us

> **Last Updated:** 2026-10-25
> **Project:** Aroh
> **Status:** Draft

## Executive Summary

Aroh has no paying customers today, and this section says so first. What it has is the raw material for evidence: a working product used daily inside one company, measured usage from 15 people, and ten friendly companies — earning ₹10–300 Cr a year — willing to try it [[1]](#ref-1), [[2]](#ref-2).

The category's central claim — that owners already feel the problem of being the integration layer — is supported by outside evidence rather than by Aroh's own customers. Indian owners in a field study worked 68-hour weeks and held decisions close because they lacked information to delegate [[5]](#ref-5); two-thirds of Indian CEOs worry about keeping pace with technology and AI [[6]](#ref-6). Aroh's job in its first year is to turn that outside evidence into its own: owners who open the 07:30 brief every day, act on it, and pay for it.

The plan for doing that is specific and dated. **Ten free pilots finish by December 2026; fifty more owners sign paid contracts by March 2027** — about 57 paying customers before the first full financial year [[3]](#ref-3). Each pilot measures the same handful of numbers, and a discovery programme with 50–100 promoters replaces the low-confidence filters in the market sizing with data.

## Owners already feel the problem — the evidence is outside Aroh today

Category design asks whether customers recognise the problem before they see the product. The outside evidence says Indian owners do:

- **Owners are stretched and can't delegate.** In a randomised study of Indian firms, owners averaged 68 hours a week; decisions stayed within the family because owners did not trust outsiders, and lack of information was a primary reason better practices were not adopted [[5]](#ref-5).
- **CEOs name the anxiety.** 66% of Indian CEOs are concerned about keeping pace with technology and AI, against 42% of CEOs globally naming it their top concern [[6]](#ref-6).
- **Owners already pay for partial fixes.** An owner's office typically carries ₹21.6–₹31.7L a year in executive assistants, MIS staff and analysts (Section 02).

What Aroh does not yet have is **behaviour from its own buyers** — Rory Sutherland's distinction between what people say and what they do [[7]](#ref-7). The pilots are designed to capture behaviour.

## The pipeline: ten friendly companies, with a caveat

**The caveat.** At ₹18L a year, Aroh would cost a ₹10 Cr company 1.8% of its revenue. The smaller friendly firms are valuable as pilots, references and product feedback, but the plan does not count on them as paying customers. Paying pilots are qualified to the ₹100 Cr+ profile.

## The fifty contracts rest on relationships DEWiN has already built

The base case assumes fifty owners sign paid contracts between January and March 2027. The founders' confidence rests on two things:

1. **Existing relationships.** DEWiN has already built relationships with owners and leaders in its network and will use them to bring owners into the first pilots and contracts.
2. **Ease of adoption and word of mouth.** The founders expect Owner Intelligence to be adopted more easily and more widely than a CRM or ERP, with owners recommending it to peers in leadership and founder circles (Section 04).

Both are **founder-stated and unproven** until contracts exist. To turn them into evidence before the pre-launch period ends, the plan adds a **named-prospect list**: every owner expected to sign, with the relationship owner, the date of the last conversation, the loop they would start with and a stated probability. The list is reviewed monthly from November 2026 and shared with any investor before the seed.

## Every pilot measures the same five numbers

A six-week pilot runs on one business loop — receivables, key accounts, order exceptions or project milestones — chosen with the owner [[3]](#ref-3).

| Measure | What "working" looks like | Why it matters |
| ------- | ------------------------- | -------------- |
| **Daily brief opens** | Owner opens the 07:30 brief on most working days | The habit is the product (Section 04) |
| **Items acted on** | Owner approves or delegates at least one item most days | Proof the brief surfaces what matters |
| **Engineer-hours to go live** | ≤ 20 | Onboarding economics (Section 08) |
| **AI requests per user per day** | Within the measured range (0.1×–1× of 432) | Cost to serve (Section 07) |
| **Conversion to a paid contract** | ₹6L setup + ₹18L annual | Willingness to pay |

At the end of each pilot the owner is also asked one Sutherland-style question about feeling, not features: *"Do you worry less about what you might be missing?"* — the psychological job Owner Intelligence exists to do (Section 00).

## Discovery with 50–100 promoters replaces assumptions with data

The market sizing flagged three filters with no public data: how many businesses are standalone rather than group subsidiaries, how many are in the launch sectors, and how many run systems Aroh can watch (Section 02). A structured discovery programme answers all three while building the sales pipeline:

| Question | Replaces |
| -------- | -------- |
| Is the business standalone or part of a group? | Standalone filter (50–80% assumed) |
| Which sector, and which systems (ERP, CRM, email, WhatsApp Business)? | Sector and digital-maturity filters |
| What does the owner's office cost today? | Budget-pool estimate (Layer 2) |
| Would you pay ₹1.5L a month for the 07:30 brief and follow-through? | Pricing confidence |

### Key Insights

- No paying customers yet; the product runs in production with measured usage (15 users, up to 432 AI requests a day) [[1]](#ref-1), [[2]](#ref-2).
- The evidence that owners feel the problem is external and strong: 68-hour weeks, information as the barrier to delegation [[5]](#ref-5), and CEO concern about keeping pace [[6]](#ref-6).
- Ten friendly companies are the starting pipeline; smaller ones serve as references, not revenue.
- Every pilot measures brief opens, actions, onboarding hours, usage and conversion.

### Investment Implications

The evidence arrives fast and on a date: ten pilots by December 2026 and fifty paid contracts by March 2027 will show whether owners open the brief, act on it and pay for it. The existing owners fund that period themselves (Section 09), so outside investors are asked to price the company only after the evidence is in — not to believe in traction that does not yet exist.
