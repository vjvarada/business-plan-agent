# Revenue Model: One Price That Says "This Runs Your Business"

> **Last Updated:** 2026-10-25
> **Project:** Aroh
> **Status:** Draft — generated from the revenue model by `write_section07.py`

## Executive Summary

Aroh sells **Owner Intelligence** to the owner, not seats to employees, and its price is built to say so. There is one tier — **Command, at ₹1.5L per organisation per month** — billed annually upfront at ₹18L, with a ₹6L setup fee at signing [[1]](#ref-1). Ten executive users are included; extra executive seats cost ₹10,000 a month, and contracts rise 7% a year at renewal.

The plan starts fast. Ten free pilots run by December 2026, and fifty more owners sign paid contracts by March 2027 — about **57 paying customers before the first full financial year** [[1]](#ref-1). From there the base case grows to 150, 300 and 500 customers at the end of FY2027–28, FY2028–29 and FY2029–30, earning **₹25.38 Cr, ₹52.59 Cr and ₹95.76 Cr** of revenue and leaving FY2029–30 with **₹106.3 Cr of annual recurring revenue** [[2]](#ref-2). Existing customers pay more each year: net revenue retention is about **109%** after 10% annual churn.

The economics hold at the extremes, not only on average. A customer's AI and production-infrastructure cost is about ₹12,400 a month at average usage — **92% gross margin before people costs** — and stays at a quarter of the price even if every user works twice as hard as today's busiest user [[3]](#ref-3).

## The price is a signal before it is a number

Rory Sutherland's argument is that price tells the buyer what kind of thing they are buying [[7]](#ref-7). A per-seat price around the cost of a phone plan says "tool". An organisation-level price says "this runs your business".

| Signal | How the price carries it |
| ------ | ------------------------ |
| **This is for the owner** | Priced per organisation, not per employee; one tier, everything included |
| **This is serious** | ₹1.5L a month and a ₹6L setup fee — the same order as the people it works alongside |
| **We are confident** | One price, no discount ladder; the free pilot is the single concession |

**What Aroh is compared against matters.** Priced against software, ₹1.5L a month looks expensive: Microsoft 365 Copilot for ten executives costs about ₹25,000 a month [[8]](#ref-8). Priced against the owner's office, it looks modest:

| What the owner pays for today | Per month |
| ----------------------------- | --------: |
| Microsoft 365 Copilot, 10 executives (₹2,495 × 10) [[8]](#ref-8) | ₹24,950 |
| **Aroh Command** | **₹1,50,000** |
| Owner's office: executive assistant + MIS executive + business analyst + Copilot seats [[9]](#ref-9) | ₹1,80,000 |
| Owner's office with a chief of staff instead of an analyst [[9]](#ref-9) | ₹2,64,000 |
| Value at stake: profit gain from better management, per firm [[10]](#ref-10) | ₹18,20,000 |

Aroh costs less than the office already built around the owner — and works across all of it — while going after a problem worth about twelve times its price.

## One tier, everything included: Command

| Element | Terms |
| ------- | ----- |
| **Subscription** | ₹1.5L per organisation per month, billed annually upfront (₹18L/year) |
| **Included** | 10 executive users; all connectors and features; 07:30 brief and 18:30 close-out; approvals; India-hosted processing |
| **Extra executive seats** | ₹10,000 per seat per month |
| **Setup fee** | ₹6L, invoiced at signing and recognised over the first contract year (onboarding takes about 20 engineer-hours) |
| **Free pilot** | Six weeks on one business loop — the route in from FY2027–28 |
| **Renewal** | Annual; contract value rises 7% at each renewal |
| **Billing** | Annual invoice, paid by bank transfer |
| **Pricing rule** | No discounts on the list price — early customers included |

**Why annual upfront.** It strengthens cash and avoids RBI's ₹15,000 limit on automatic recurring debits without extra authentication, which a ₹1.5L monthly charge would exceed [[4]](#ref-4).

## The base case reaches ₹106.3 Cr of ARR by March 2030

**How the pre-launch period is shown.** It is a five-month stub (November 2026–March 2027), not a financial year, and it is not annualised. The ten free pilots end by December; about 7 convert and are live for roughly two months by 31 March. The fifty direct customers are onboarded during March, so each is live for only about half a month. Contracts are billed annually upfront, so the cash arrives in March while revenue is recognised over the following twelve months — subscription month by month, and the ₹6L setup fee spread over the first contract year. That is why pre-launch **recognised revenue is only ₹0.78 Cr while ₹13.68 Cr is billed**; the difference sits in deferred revenue and flows into FY2027–28. Each customer's first contract year runs from its signing date, so most pre-launch customers renew near the end of FY2027–28. Later customers sign evenly through each year [[2]](#ref-2).

| Base case | Pre-launch* | FY2027–28 | FY2028–29 | FY2029–30 |
| --------- | ------: | ------: | ------: | ------: |
| Customers, end of period | 57 | 150 | 300 | 500 |
| New customers | 57 | 99 | 165 | 230 |
| Free pilots run | 10 | 141 | 236 | 329 |
| Direct paid onboardings | 50 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| Subscription revenue (₹ Cr) | 0.58 | 19.12 | 42.15 | 76.14 |
| Extra-seat revenue (₹ Cr) | 0.00 | 0.08 | 2.53 | 7.78 |
| Setup fees, recognised (₹ Cr) | 0.20 | 6.19 | 7.91 | 11.85 |
| **Total revenue (₹ Cr)** | **0.78** | **25.38** | **52.59** | **95.76** |
| **Annual recurring revenue, end of period (₹ Cr)** | **10.26** | **28.96** | **61.14** | **106.29** |
| Billings: cash invoiced in the period (₹ Cr) | 13.68 | 34.89 | 71.04 | 120.09 |
| Deferred revenue, end of period (₹ Cr) | 12.90 | 22.40 | 40.86 | 65.19 |

*Pre-launch is a five-month stub period (November 2026–March 2027), not a financial year, and is not annualised. Its figures are recognised revenue and cost for those five months only; annual contracts signed in March are billed upfront but recognised over the following twelve months.

**Reconciliation with the source strategy document** [[5]](#ref-5): it projected ₹3.0 / ₹14.1 / ₹40.2 Cr on a 20 / 90 / 250 ramp — essentially this plan's downside. The base case is roughly twice as large because it starts with ~57 paying customers before April 2027 and grows to 500.

**What the base case needs to be true:**

- **Fifty owners sign ₹24L first-year contracts in January–March 2027** on the strength of ten pilots and the founders' networks — the plan's single most important assumption.
- About 12 free pilots a month in FY2027–28, rising to about 27 a month in FY2029–30.
- 500 customers is 6.0% of the base serviceable market — above this plan's 5% red-flag line — and 3.5% of the broad SAM; the discovery programme (Section 05) must validate the larger market before the seed.

## The economics hold at the extremes, not only on average

Sutherland's warning — don't design for the average — applies to AI costs, which depend on how hard people use the product [[7]](#ref-7). Variable cost per organisation (10 users: AI calls plus production infrastructure) at measured rates [[3]](#ref-3):

| Usage | AI + production infrastructure per org/month | Share of ₹1.5L price | Gross margin before people costs |
| ----- | -------------------------: | -------------------: | -------------------------------: |
| Typical user (0.1×) | ₹4,700 | 3% | 97% |
| Average active day (0.55×) | ₹12,400 | 8% | **92%** |
| Busiest user today (1.0×) | ₹20,200 | 13% | 87% |
| Twice the busiest user (2.0×) | ₹37,300 | 25% | 75% |

The figures include ₹3,000 a month per customer of production infrastructure — about 3.4 times the ₹880 measured today — — compute, RAM, storage, bandwidth, backups and monitoring — on top of AI calls. Extra seats add AI cost only (about ₹1,000 a month against a ₹10,000 price, 90%). People costs, development infrastructure, customer acquisition cost and lifetime value are in Section 08.

### Key Insights

- One tier, ₹1.5L per organisation per month, signals an executive product; it is anchored against the owner's office (₹1.8–₹2.6L a month), not against per-seat software [[8]](#ref-8), [[9]](#ref-9).
- Base case: ~57 paying customers by March 2027, 500 by March 2030; revenue ₹25.4 / ₹52.6 / ₹95.8 Cr; **₹106.3 Cr exit ARR** [[2]](#ref-2).
- Net revenue retention of ~109% comes from a 7% uplift and extra seats, despite 10% churn.
- Variable gross margin is 92% at average usage and still 77% at twice the busiest user's usage [[3]](#ref-3).

### Investment Implications

The revenue model makes Aroh an organisation-level software business with expanding customers, not a seat reseller with thin margins. Each customer is worth more every year it stays, and AI costs are a small and falling share of the price. The variable that decides everything is the first one: whether fifty owners sign by March 2027. The plan is built so that this is proven before outside investors are asked to price the company.
